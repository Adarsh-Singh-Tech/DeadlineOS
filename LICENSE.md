## Why MIT for DeadlineOS

MIT is one of the most widely adopted and permissive open source licenses. It was chosen for its simplicity and maximum openness:

| Comparison | MIT Advantage |
|---|---|
| **vs Apache 2.0** | Simpler and shorter; no patent grant clauses; widely recognized and trusted by the developer community |
| **vs Custom Portfolio License** | Standard licenses reduce legal friction, increase ecosystem familiarity, and signal engineering credibility to recruiters and contributors |
| **vs Proprietary** | Enables public showcase value and developer trust while preserving attribution and ownership clarity |

**The founder positioning:** MIT provides maximum openness and simplicity — ideal for a portfolio repository where broad adoption and minimal friction matter most. The only requirement is retaining the copyright notice.

## Founder Positioning Note

If this public repository is intended to showcase engineering quality while a future commercial product — including hosted execution, proprietary workflows, managed services, customer data, and production infrastructure — remains independently controlled, MIT works well for this separation.

This is particularly suited where:

- The repository is a **product engineering case study**
- The SaaS business model depends on **hosted execution** rather than raw source exclusivity
- Brand, customer workflows, datasets, and deployment systems remain **outside the public repo scope**
- Maximum adoption and **minimal legal friction** are priorities

If the goal later shifts to stricter commercialization control — including patent protection — Apache 2.0 or a source-available model can be adopted for future versions.
